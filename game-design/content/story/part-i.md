# Zanzibar's World of Energy

## Day 1

### Opening Scene

Zanzibar is walking alone in the forest. It is early afternoon on a nice summer day. The sun is still high, the weather pleasant. A gentle warm breeze meets his face. The natural pine scent keeps Zanzibar alert.

He does not need to check his backpack to know he is low on provisions. His stomach makes it clear that he will need to find something more to eat before too much longer.

Zanzibar is fueled by sheer optimism. At his most desperate moments, he thinks, "Things will be better. I will survive."

About a day before, he and his small group of travelers reach a fork in the path. The others think it best to head toward the east, while something inside of Zanzibar tells him to go west. They cannot agree on a direction, but they know the must keep moving. Splitting up is a calculated risk.

Unfortunately for Zanzibar, what starts as a path dwindles to more of a suggestion. At least the mature pines leave a lot of open space that makes it easy to pass. The ground is carpeted with dropped needles and small branches.

At this point, turning back would be a mistake.

At last, Zanzibar notices something new. A wall of vines grows out of the ground ahead. The wall stretches in a straight line up and down the slope of the mountain, completely cutting off his progress to the west.

Closer inspection reveals that the vines are covering a chain link fence. At the bottom, the fence is buried. Along the top, sharp coils of wire and regular spikes make it clear that one is not to go over.

Zanzibar wants to find out what this fence is keeping in.

Zanzibar turns uphill, hoping that higher ground will offer a vantage point. As he walks, he considers the fence and what is on the other side.

When he reaches a corner, he rounds the bend. Before long, he can see a paved road and a little guard house. His pace quickens.

At last, things are getting interesting. Zanzibar discovers a little guard house where someone must have sat to examine the paperwork of visitors to the facility. The road slides under the fence at this point, where a closed gate is blocking the entrance.

The guard house is closed tight. Peeking in the windows makes it plain that it is also empty. No point in breaking in.

Zanzibar takes a moment to relax while he thinks about what to do. He nibbles at his last energy bar and sips some water. Something about the gate seems off.

On closer inspection, Zanzi notices that Mother Nature has taken the security of the premises into her hands. Vines have wrapped around the posts of the gate and fence, holding the gate closed.

Zanzi unravels the vines, slide the gate open just enough to slip through and replaces the vines as he found. The illusion of security is better than nothing.

He heads down the road.

### The Compound

The road is in decent shape. It is obscured by years of pine needle drops. In addition to the pine trees, the area has shrubs and a few thickets. As he walks, Zanzibar begins to notice the white noise of falling water. There must be a nearby stream or river.

Zanzibar is curious what he will find along the road.

Just ahead is a gravel driveway that joins the road at the right and runs off toward the water sounds. Zanzi turns down the driveway, walks past a few more trees into a clearing. The driveway comes to a building, some kind of outpost or ranger station. Further to the west of the building is the source of the white noise, a section of rapids on what is either a large stream or a small river.

The building features a large industrial-sized garage with two roll-up doors. One side is more of a regular door, one story high and wide enough to fit a car or pickup truck. The other side is impressive. Two stories high, almost twice as wide as the smaller door. Large enough to fit construction equipment.

A stone wall lines the western edge of the driveway, keeping vehicles from falling over the riverbank. Stone stairs sit between the building and the wall for easy access by foot to the cascade.

A walkway wraps around to the other side of the garage.

Zanzibar wants to get inside the building. Seeing no way to open the roll-up doors, Zanzibar follows the path to a man door at the side.

### The Garage

It is no wonder that the side door is locked. Whoever left this area might have realized they would never return. This seems an ideal place to take shelter and to make sense of things. Zanzibar has to get in.

Zanzibar has bested more formidable locks. Still, he would prefer to leave it functional. For now, getting in is more important.

He steps into the larger garage bay, which is rather empty. Tire marks and oil stains make it clear that some large equipment is parked here at some point. Directly opposite is a staircase to a door on the second story. Above the stairs is a row of grimy windows. Around the perimeter are strong posts supporting beams. A cross beam has a set of pulleys and chains hanging down. Zanzibar imagines that this rigging could help to unload heavy objects.

There are stairs along the side wall to a door on the second story.

While Zanzibar is taking that in, something in the next bay catches his eye. He walks over to get a closer look.

This side of the garage has a tool bench, charging cables, and a power panel. Although it is covered, the lump in the middle of this garage bay has the distinctive shape of a vehicle. He pealed back the cover to reveal a stylish dune buggy with shiny chrome and knobby wheels.

As much fun as that looks, Zanzibar needs to find some food. He replaces the cover and continues his self-guided tour. There is a door in the back wall. Locked. Zanzibar decides to see if the stairs will provide for a more civilized entry.

### The Conference Room

Zanzibar climbs the stairs to a bit of good luck. The door is unlocked, so he goes through into a stylish conference room, formal without being stuffy. The decor fit the surroundings, with a large, rustic wooden table circled by a number of sturdy wooden chairs.

The sides were lined with windows, those that overlooked the garage, and on the opposite side, larger windows to what must be a spectacular view. They needed cleaning, inside and out.

The wall at the end of the conference room featured a pair of wooden panels. Zanzibar slide the panels apart to reveal a large screen. Presentations, conference calls, movie night. From what Zanzibar had seen so far, he could tell that whoever stayed here was doing more than camping. This is a serious facility. What happened?

A second door stood at the back of the room. The door was unlocked. As Zanzibar opened it, adrenaline flooded his body with the instant recognition of what he had found.

## Day 2

Zanzibar opens his eyes to a thin slice of light that is coming through the riverside wall. He realizes that fancy wooden shutters are closed, obscuring most of the windows. He slides them open to an amazing ground-level view of the waterfall and the sloping riverbank. He also notices that a pipe extends the length of the falls from the top to a point just below where he stands.

What an exciting day. Three days ago, he makes a bold decision based on a hunch. Today, he will continue to explore this outpost that is more than a rustic getaway. While the library might have some answers, his first priority is to answer the call of Mother Nature. Time to visit the room next to the library.

It is a small miracle that the facilities are in working order. Running water, a supply of paper products, even an old bar of soap. A welcome improvement after days of roughing it.

The next priority is to find breakfast. Zanzibar goes back up the spiral staircase, enjoying the sight and sound of the cascade. He is impressed that the flow is still rather strong. It has been at least a month since the last of the snow vanished from the mountain summit.

Back in the kitchen, Zanzibar rifles through boxes of Tasty Tack. He opens a box of Pioneer Breakfast, "a complete meal, with griddle cakes, eggs and bacon." Each pack has three bars with their distinctive flavors. Zanzibar takes small bites, trying each on its own, then in combination. He washes it down with a fresh batch of purified river water.

After a satisfying meal, Zanzibar steps back into the spiral stairwell. The doors at the top and bottom are on springloaded hinges, and with both doors closed and steady the sound of the rushing water, the effect is hypnotic. Zanzibar feels a sense of deep calm that he has not experienced in quite somem time. Out of this calm rises an idea. Everything is going to work out.

Ready for the day, Zanzibar returns to the library to continue his self-guided tour.

### The Library

One would think that a library in the middle of a mountainside forest would have books about nature or philosophy. Thoreau would fit right in. Perhaps a Theodore Roosevelt biography. Sure enough, a number of large picture books featuring natural scenes grace one of the shelves.

The vast majority of material in this library is straight out of any elite science and engineering school. The titles are best-in-show spanning a wide variety of topics.

Zanzibar has to make do with natural lighting from the expansive windows, which is sufficient on a sunny day. The lack of power means that he is unable to use anything electrical, such as the pair of holo-readers in the library.

Holo-reader technology produces an immersive 3D experience and is designed for enhanced intake of information. It is a step above reading. Through rapid flow-state triggering, people have the sensation of disappearing into their own skulls while exploring any given topic of interest.

The people at this station are into science and engineering. Between the pair of holo-readers is a rack of infopods that mirrors the physical books.

- Fundamentals of Physics
- Partical Physics
- Chemistry: Potions, Reactions, Surprises
- Mechanical Engineering, the Complete Works
- Electro-magnetism, Maxwell's Faraday Cage Match
- Quantum Machanics - Eigen see your nano banana
- Fluid Dynamics
- Statistical Thermodynamics
- Hydro Power, Water You Waiting For?
- Photovoltaics, PVs in Charge
- Battery Storage Through the Years
- Nuclear Fission
- Power Grids

There are more, as well as a collection of games and virtual vacations.

With the power out, these materials are inaccessible. To make the most of this library, Zanzibar needs to find a way to turn on the power.

### The Control Room

The library has two doors. Zanzibar opens the one he has yet to try and finds a corridor that slopes downhill. This passage leads to another door that opens into some kind of control room. It too has a wall of windows as in the library. Zanzibar slides open the shutters to let in light and take in the view from this lower vantage point. Now he is near the bottom of the cascade.

Outside, the pipe ends at a small building just below.

Turning and scanning the room, everything is covered as if the painters would be arriving today. Except it has probably been this way for years. Zanzibar pulls back the large sheet over the lump in the center of the room. He finds a sizable desk area built into the floor with monitors and keyboards on top.

On one side of the room is a closed closet. At the back are two doors: one goes to a restroom, and the other to a lobby that buffers the room from elements and visitors of the outside world.

A binder stands tall between the monitors. Zanzibar reads the title on its spine: _Micro-Hydro Power Generator: Operational Guide_. He opens the binder and finds a laminated card in the front pocket. On one side of the card are illustrated step-by-step instructions for turning on the micro-hydro generator. The pictures are from points along the pipe outside.

These steps look rather simple.

1. Clear and open the intake.
2. Walk to the mid-section. Turn the bypass valve so that water flows down the pipe.
3. Look up and down the pipe for obvious leaks. Determine if one or more patches is needed.
4. Walk to the base of the pipe. Open the pressure valve, and check the water pressure.
5. Once the water pressure is high enough (above 60 PSI), open valve to power housing. You should hear the rising pitch of the turbine spinning up.
6. Go back to the control room. Open the electrical panel, and connect the switch to the micro-generator.
7. Turn on some lights, fire up a terminal, and enjoy the miracle of electricity.

The set-up could not be more perfect. With nothing to lose and no reason to hesitate, Zanzibar heads for the lobby, unlocks the outside door and steps out into the cool morning.

### A Miracle Occurs

Fortunately he is in for another day of fabulous weather. Light clouds, gentle breeze, sunlight filtered by pine needles. He sees the back of the garage uphill from where he stands with a path leading to the door he entered the day before.

In the other direction, the path turns along the outer wall of the control room toward the river. On the bank stands a sort of shed with a pipe and gauges sticking out of the uphill side. This must be the power housing. The path transitions from decomposed granite to solid granite of the river bank. An occasional step is carved into the solid rock, making it safe to follow up the bank, past stairwell and to the upper corner of the building.

At the corner, a stone staircase is wedged between the building and the stone retaining wall that he saw at the edge of the driveway. Zanzibar's attention turns to the river. He sees a submerged concrete cube with a metal lid. He manipulates a lever at the bank that is attached to the cover, and it slides to the side. He hears water filling the pipe, and before long, water is pouring out of a pipe below at what must be the mid-section.

Time for step 2. Zanzi returns along the path to the mid-section. There is a handwheel like you might find on a submarine for sealing a hatch. In this case, the wheel is connected to a valve with two positions. The position it is in diverts water back into the cascade. Step 2 is to turn the valve to direct the water down the pipe. Zanzibar flexes to overcome the relunctant valve. With a jerk it comes loose. It moves into place with a few turns of the wheel.

Step 3 is to check for leaks. He scans up and down. No spraying, no gushing, just a few slow drips.

The next step is to check the pressure. He walks back to the power housing and studies the gizmos attached to the pipe that goes through the side. A pressure gauge is attached to the end of a short pipe with a valve. The gauge is a 0. Zanzi opens the valve. The pressure jumps to about 45 PSI and is rising. All good signs.

Before long, the pressure reaches 60 PSI. Zanzibar grabs the nob for the valve to the housing and turns it slowly. He can hear the water push through. A whirring sound starts low and increases in pitch and intensity as he opens the valve completely. Water starts to pour out of the pipe below the housing back into the river, at this point below the cascade.

So far so good. This is almost too good to be true. He has one more step to see it all come together. Apparently this has to be done from inside the control room. Zanzibar walks back around the building to the lobby and into the control room. He goes to the wall with the power panel, finds the switch conveniently labeled micro-hydro, and flips it on.

The main terminal on the console flickers on. Within seconds, a prompt appears.
